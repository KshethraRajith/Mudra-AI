Bharatanatyam Hasta Mudras

Ancient Language of Hands from the Abhinaya Darpana

In classical Indian dance, Hastas (hand gestures) form a sacred visual alphabet. Rooted in the Natya Shastra and codified in Nandikeshvara's Abhinaya Darpana, the single-hand gestures (Asamyuta Hastas) convey poetry, mythology, nature, and divine emotion.

Pataaka Asamyuta Hasta #1

"Nāṭyārambhe vāridhare vane vastuniṣedhane..."

All five fingers are extended straight and held close together, with the thumb gently bent touching the edge of the palm. Used at the beginning of a dance performance, to depict clouds, dense forests, forbidding/stopping, night, river, and divine blessings.

Tripataka Asamyuta Hasta #2

"Makuṭe vṛkṣabhāve ca vajre tattaddhare tathā..."

Derived from Pataaka by bending down only the ring finger. Signifies a royal crown (makuta), a sacred tree, Indra's thunderbolt (vajra), the third part of a flag, arrows, or holy lamps.

Ardhapataaka Asamyuta Hasta #3

"Pallave phalake tīre ubhayoriti vācakaḥ..."

From Tripataka, the little finger is also bent down alongside the ring finger. Signifies tender tender leaves (pallava), writing boards, temple towers, animal horns, or the twin banks of a river.

Kartari Mukham Asamyuta Hasta #4

"Strīpuṁsayostu viśleṣe viparyāsapade'pi ca..."

The index and middle fingers are spread apart like scissor blades, while the thumb joins the bent ring and little fingers. Depicts the pain of separation between lovers, sudden lightning, the outer corners of eyes, or a fall.

Mayuram Asamyuta Hasta #5

"Mayūragalake lekhāyāṁ śakune yukte..."

The tip of the ring finger touches the tip of the thumb, while other fingers are upright. Depicts the graceful neck of a dancing peacock, applying the auspicious tilak/bindi to the forehead, good omens, or stroking hair.
